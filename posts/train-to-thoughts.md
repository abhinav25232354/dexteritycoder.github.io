# Train to Thoughts

I named this section after the specific quality of thinking that happens when you are moving and have nothing to do but look out a window.

On a train, you are suspended between places. You are not yet where you are going and no longer where you came from. The motion is constant but you are not doing anything to cause it. There is nothing to manage, nothing to decide, no input to process. Your mind, freed from the obligation of task completion, begins to do something different. It starts making connections it would not make under the pressure of purposeful thinking. It starts following threads that have no obvious destination. It starts asking questions it would normally defer because there is no immediately useful answer.

This is the kind of thinking I want to share here. Not polished arguments with conclusions already decided before the writing began. Not content produced to demonstrate expertise or to be algorithmically palatable. Just thinking, conducted in public, in the imperfect and exploratory way that real thinking actually works.

## What This Section Is

This is my blog, but I resist that word because it implies a regularity and a format that I do not intend to impose on this writing. A blog suggests a publication schedule, a niche, a consistent voice calibrated for an audience. Train to Thoughts is more like a collection of dispatches from wherever my mind happened to be going on a particular day.

Some of these pieces will be about technology, because technology is where I spend most of my waking attention. Some will be about building things and what building things does to your relationship with the world. Some will be about aesthetics, about what makes a piece of music or a film or a piece of code feel inevitable rather than arbitrary. Some will be philosophical in a way that is not academic, meaning I will think seriously about questions without pretending I am equipped to produce definitive answers. Some will be personal in a way that is not confessional, meaning I will share my actual experience of navigating the things I navigate without making the sharing itself the point.

I am interested in the intersection of engineering and philosophy because I think that intersection is where the most interesting questions live. What does it mean to understand something? What is the relationship between the precision required by formal systems and the irreducible ambiguity of human experience? What does the act of building things do to your conception of agency and authorship? These are not questions with clean answers. They are questions worth sitting with.

I am interested in the aesthetics of effort, in what distinguishes work that feels like it came from somewhere real versus work that feels assembled. I am interested in the experience of being a learner in a field that changes faster than anyone can fully track, and in the particular kind of intellectual honesty that requires. I am interested in what it means to build a small business from nothing and to have that business depend entirely on systems you wrote yourself, and in the combination of exhilaration and responsibility that produces.

## What I Am Not Trying to Do Here

I am not trying to build a personal brand through this writing. I am not trying to produce content that performs well on distribution algorithms. I am not trying to establish myself as a thought leader, which is a phrase I find philosophically suspect because it implies that thoughts are a kind of territory to be claimed.

I am trying to think clearly, in writing, about things that seem worth thinking about. If that is useful to you, I am glad. If it is occasionally uncomfortable or uncertain or inconclusive, that is not a failure of the writing. That is what honest thinking looks like.

## How Often I Write

When I have something worth saying. Not on a schedule. The pieces that get published here will be pieces where I felt, at the end of writing them, that the thinking had genuinely moved somewhere. The ones where I was going through motions or producing words without adding understanding will stay in drafts, or get deleted, or get rewritten until they are something I can stand behind.

I would rather publish twelve pieces in a year that I am proud of than fifty-two pieces that exist only because a week passed.

The name is an invitation. Come think with me. Bring your own window.
